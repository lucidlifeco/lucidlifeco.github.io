The standard advice

Why the usual fixes don't work

When money feels tight, the standard advice is more. More sales. More ads. More posts. A new website. More hours.

  • More sales

    More revenue through the same leaks means more unpaid invoices, more thin-margin jobs and more work for the same result. And you can't spend revenue. You can only spend what's left.

  • More marketing

    It sends more people into the same gaps. If inquiries wait a day for a reply, twice the inquiries means twice the lost jobs. You pay for the lead and lose it anyway.

  • More hours

    You can't outwork a leak. There are only so many hours in a week, and you're already using them.

  • Lower prices

    They win the job and give away the margin. You end up busier and no better off.

  • More outside help

    Most of it is measured on leads, clicks and posts. Almost none of it is measured on what you keep.

Pouring more water into a leaky bucket just means paying for more leaks.

A better order

Fix the bucket first.

Same customers. Same team. Same hours. More of the money stays.

  1. First

    Plug the leaks

    Faster replies, open quotes, overdue invoices, old prices, past customers and referrals. None of it needs an ad budget.

  2. Then

    Make the fixes stick

    Turn them into simple routines that run without anyone having to remember: the words to use, a weekly rhythm and a clear owner for each.

  3. Then

    Turn up the marketing

    Once every inquiry is worth more, marketing becomes an investment and stops being a gamble.

  • It's money you already earned, so you don't wait for new customers to find you.
  • It costs time, not ad spend.
  • It makes every dollar of marketing you spend later go further.
