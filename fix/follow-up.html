The 3-5-7 Follow-Up Plan

Most quotes that go quiet aren't a no. The customer got busy, got a second opinion, or forgot. Three short messages bring a lot of them back.

3messages per open quote
5minutes or less each
7days, start to finish

The three messages

Send them by text unless the customer asked for email. Swap in the words in [brackets], and keep the rest as is.

Day 3 · The question
Hi [Name], it's [Your name] from [Business]. I had a question about the [job] quote. Is the timing still right for you, or has something changed?

A question gets a reply. "Just checking in" gets ignored.

Day 5 · The useful detail
Hi [Name]. One thing I didn't mention: [something useful, like an opening next week, or that waiting will make the problem worse]. Want me to hold that for you?

Give them a real reason to decide now. Never invent urgency. If there isn't a real reason, skip to day 7.

Day 7 · The close-out
Hi [Name]. I haven't heard back, so I'm guessing the [job] isn't a priority right now. Should I close out your quote?

This one gets the most replies. People answer a question that's easy to say no to, and many of them say "No, don't close it, let's go."

Five rules that make it work

  • Send every message. Most of the wins come from day 7.
  • Stop the moment they reply, yes or no.
  • If they say no, ask one question: "Mind if I ask what made you decide?" Write the answer down. It's free pricing advice.
  • Same time of day each time. Early evening works for most homeowners.
  • Never discount to get a reply. Lead with a question, not a lower price.
